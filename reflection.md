Making this interactive microarchitecture timing attack reinforced how I understand the hidden security implications of hardware optimization. Even though we went over them in lecture and how CPU caches and timing attacks work theoretically, designing and reviewing an interactive model helped me better understand the exact mechanics of the vulnerability and attack. I understood that latency gaps between caches and RAM can leak secret data. Moreover, simulating the flush + reload attack cycle helped me better understand how the data was taken from the victim. This instilled in me why it is crucial to have constant-time algorithms, even if they are slower, to have better cryptographic resilience. My development workflow relied on an iterative AI-assisted approach to bring my vision to life. I started by giving Gemini my UI requirments and core michroarchitecture concepts into Gemini to create a self contained index.html. Then I refined the app by adding better visuals and descriptions of the attack process. I then verified the timing calculations made sense and were realistic . Finally, I used Gemini to create a Read.me to explain the project and how to run it. Ultimately, this project to explain this attack to others solidified my understanding of it and gave me a deeper understanding of why constant-time programming and microarchitecture security matter.
